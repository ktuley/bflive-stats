Summary

Viewers left 20,350 comments on the 15 YouTube and 14 Facebook live shows, from about 3,270 accounts. Three in four of the posters who named a location were outside New Mexico. Between them they named every state except Rhode Island, plus 22 other countries.

  • A national and international audience of past and future visitors. About 270 posters say they have been to Fiesta, about 150 want to come, and about 170 say they plan to.
  • The hosts are a big reason people come back. Art, Ted and new co-host Dylan are mentioned more than 750 times between them, almost always warmly.
  • What viewers want most is status. One in fourteen comments is a question, and most ask about flags, weather, the glow or the fireworks.
  • Complaints are rare. About 50 comments mention stream problems and about 90 voice disappointment, mostly over wind cancellations.

Missing Fiesta is a strong pull. Many past visitors and former New Mexicans watch because they can’t be there this year. About 120 comments mention “next year,” and several ask practical questions about multi-day tickets, which days to go and what to expect.

What viewers liked

The hosts and the simple chance to watch drew the most praise, followed by the special shapes and the glows.

  1. The hosts. Art is mentioned about 330 times, Ted about 250 and Dylan about 175 (often spelled “Dillon”). Dylan gets warm notes: “his enthusiasm makes it even more exciting,” “will be a broadcaster for sure.” About 30 comments mention the Rocky Mountain Emmy nomination, and a few thank the team for not using AI.
  2. Being able to watch at all. “Thank you for broadcasting this every year so we can see this,” “for folks who love balloons but hate crowds.” About 90 comments mention watching with a classroom, students, kids or grandkids; one kindergarten class watches every morning.
  3. Special shapes and named balloons. Airabelle the cow leads by far, with about 140 mentions. Next come Boo, Scout, Finley the turtle, Zozobra, Smokey, Darth Vader and Yoda, Montie the black sheep, Steggy, the Roadrunner and Humpty Dumpty. Viewers ask for their favorites by name and cheer when they appear.
  4. The glows. About 1,900 comments are emoji only. Most are long strings of fire and sparkle emoji posted during the “all burn” moments, as viewers count down with the hosts.
  5. The extras. Dawn Patrol, the drone show, the fireworks, the Team Fastrax skydivers and the National Anthem performances all come up. A harmonica anthem drew comment.

Method and caveats

Theme counts come from matching keywords and phrases, checked by reading samples, so they are close estimates rather than exact tallies.

  • Source. Comments were captured Oct 13–14, 2026: the full YouTube live chat replay for 15 streams, YouTube video comments, and every comment visible on the 14 Facebook live videos. Facebook’s own counts include hidden and deleted comments, so about 91% were captured there and 97% on YouTube.
  • People, not accounts. YouTube uses handles and Facebook uses names, so someone who posted on both platforms is counted twice.
  • Locations. A place was counted only when a poster named a state, city or country, usually in a greeting. “Kansas City” and two-letter abbreviations make a few state counts approximate.
  • Included. Posts by the Balloon Fiesta accounts and the hosts (about 47) are included, but they don’t change the picture. Quotes are shown without names.
